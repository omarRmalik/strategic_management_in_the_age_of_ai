## Course Description

Strategic Management in the Age of Artificial Intelligence examines how artificial intelligence is transforming competitive strategy, organizational capabilities, executive decision making, innovation, and enterprise performance. The course integrates classical strategic management theories—including industry analysis, the Resource-Based View, Dynamic Capabilities, Organizational Learning, Transaction Cost Economics, and Strategic Leadership—with contemporary scholarship on artificial intelligence, digital transformation, platform ecosystems, and human–AI collaboration.

Students critically evaluate how AI influences the assumptions underlying strategic management while developing practical competencies through case analyses, AI laboratories, executive briefings, and a semester-long consulting engagement. Emphasis is placed on evidence-based decision making, responsible AI governance, organizational transformation, and strategic leadership in increasingly AI-enabled organizations.

*For full course philosophy and rationale, see [Part 0 — Course Philosophy](https://github.com/omarRmalik/strategic_management_in_the_age_of_ai/blob/main/md%20files/part_0_course_philosophy.md) and [Part I — Course Foundations](https://github.com/omarRmalik/strategic_management_in_the_age_of_ai/blob/main/md%20files/part_1_course_foundations.md).*

## Required Materials

### Textbooks

Iansiti, M., & Lakhani, K. R. (2020). *Competing in the age of AI: Strategy and leadership when algorithms and networks run the world*. Harvard Business Review Press.

Agrawal, A., Gans, J., & Goldfarb, A. (2018). *Prediction machines: The simple economics of artificial intelligence*. Harvard Business Review Press.

Agrawal, A., Gans, J., & Goldfarb, A. (2022). *Power and prediction: The disruptive economics of artificial intelligence*. Harvard Business Review Press.

Lafley, A. G., & Martin, R. L. (2013). *Playing to win: How strategy really works*. Harvard Business Review Press.

### Required Readings

Weekly readings pair foundational strategic management scholarship with contemporary AI research. See [Part X — Learning Resources and Curated Knowledge Base](https://github.com/omarRmalik/strategic_management_in_the_age_of_ai/blob/main/md%20files/part_10_reading_list.md) for the full annotated reading list.

**Foundational Canon includes:** Porter (1996), Barney (1991), Teece, Pisano & Shuen (1997), March (1991), Grant (1996), Prahalad & Hamel (1990), Williamson (1979), Wernerfelt (1984), Teece (2007).

**Contemporary AI Canon includes:** Hutzschenreuter et al. (2025), Jarrahi (2018), Shrestha et al. (2019), Kellogg et al. (2020), Makarius et al. (2020), von Krogh (2018), and others.

### Cases

Harvard Business School cases are used on alternating weeks. Cases include:

- *AI Wars* (Week 2)
- *HubSpot and Motion AI* (Week 4)
- *Osaro: Picking the Best Path* (Week 6)
- *JPMorganChase: Leadership in the Age of GenAI* (Week 8)
- Executive Board Simulation (Week 10)

### Software

Students will use AI tools (e.g., Claude, ChatGPT, or equivalent) as part of the AI Strategy Laboratory component. No programming experience is required.

- Reliable internet connection

## Assignment Descriptions

*For complete assignment descriptions, see [Part VII — The AI Strategy Consulting Project](https://github.com/omarRmalik/strategic_management_in_the_age_of_ai/blob/main/md%20files/part_7_strategy_consulting_project.md) and [Part VI — AI Strategy Laboratories](https://github.com/omarRmalik/strategic_management_in_the_age_of_ai/blob/main/md%20files/part_6_strategy_labs.md).*

**Theory Evolution Exercises** — Ten short weekly essays responding to the prompt: *"If I were rewriting this classical theory in 2026, I would…"* Students identify underlying assumptions of classical theories, evaluate how AI challenges those assumptions, and propose theoretically grounded revisions.

**HBS Case Analyses** — Four Harvard Business School case analyses submitted on alternating weeks (Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8). Each analysis applies strategic management frameworks to real organizational AI challenges.

**AI Strategy Laboratories** — Hands-on laboratory exercises using AI tools to perform strategic analysis tasks. Labs are completed approximately every other week and develop responsible, critical AI use as a professional competency. See [Part VI](https://github.com/omarRmalik/strategic_management_in_the_age_of_ai/blob/main/md%20files/part_6_strategy_labs.md).

**AI Strategy Consulting Project** — Semester-long team consulting engagement advising an existing organization on AI strategy. Weekly milestones build toward a final consulting report and executive presentation. See [Part VII](https://github.com/omarRmalik/strategic_management_in_the_age_of_ai/blob/main/md%20files/part_7_strategy_consulting_project.md).

**Executive Board Presentation** — Teams present their final AI strategy recommendations in a simulated Board of Directors meeting during Week 10. Evaluated on strategic rigor, evidence quality, and executive communication.

## Attendance and Participation Policy

This course is discussion-intensive. Active, prepared participation is essential to the learning environment. Students are expected to attend every class session having completed all assigned readings.

Participation is evaluated on the quality of contributions to executive discussions, case debriefs, and laboratory sessions — not merely on frequency of speaking. Unexcused absences will reduce the participation grade. More than two unexcused absences may result in a grade penalty; please contact the instructor in advance if conflicts are anticipated.

## Artificial Intelligence (AI) Policy

This course views artificial intelligence as a thinking partner, not an answer generator. Students are encouraged to use AI tools to brainstorm, compare theories, summarize literature, draft outlines, and support strategic analysis.

All submitted work must reflect the student's own reasoning, judgment, and scholarly understanding. AI-generated content submitted without meaningful intellectual contribution constitutes a violation of academic integrity. Major assignments must include a brief AI Disclosure Statement describing how AI was used in the preparation of the work.

*For the complete AI policy including appropriate and inappropriate uses, see [Part IX — Responsible AI, Academic Integrity, and Professional Conduct](https://github.com/omarRmalik/strategic_management_in_the_age_of_ai/blob/main/md%20files/part_9_policies.md).*
